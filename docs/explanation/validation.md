# Validation

A retrieval needs three different checks: numerical correctness of its
calculation, adequacy of the physical measurement model, and accuracy of the
inferred field. A small fitted trace residual addresses only part of this.
This chapter explains what the available numerical evidence establishes and
where it remains limited. For everyday diagnostics, start with
[interpreting results](../user_guide/results.md).

Two associated **unpublished drafts** provide the evidence discussed here:
*Differentiable physical models for ultrashort-pulse retrieval* examines the
inverse problem; *A dispersive and spatial model of transient-grating
frequency-resolved optical gating* examines the forward model. See
[references](../reference/bibliography.md). All results below are numerical
simulations. Neither draft establishes an experimental duration or a general
accuracy guarantee for every FROG geometry.

## The virtual instrument

The reference traces are generated by a three-dimensional propagation model of
a TG-FROG instrument. A mask produces three beamlets, which are focused into a
fused-silica slab; signal in the fourth BOXCARS direction is selected by a
collection aperture. Dispersion, diffraction, nonlinear generation and spatial
collection are represented in the propagation reference, independently of the
reduced model used for retrieval.

The source pulse, integrated beamlet spectrum and on-axis slab-entrance field
are distinct. The principal comparisons use the **on-axis entrance field**.
Standard and extended retrievals use an effective one-dimensional field and
must have their complex spectra converted before comparison in time. See
[field conventions](../reference/conventions.md#field-reference-planes).
Temporal interpolation can change the reported FWHM by a few attoseconds even
without changing the complex field.

## The failure being fixed

A model can reproduce a trace closely while retrieving a substantially wrong
pulse. The simulated RDW example below gives a standard-model residual of
0.266% rms of the trace peak at 9.5 µm thickness and a 2 mm collection aperture.
After converting the complex retrieved field to the on-axis convention, its
FWHM is 2.433 fs, compared with a finely sampled reference of 1.045 fs. Its stored
native effective-field width is 2.499 fs: these are different conventions, not
different retrievals.

## The three forward models

| Model | Included physics | Important limits |
|---|---|---|
| Standard | Local, thin-medium interaction | Neglects evolution and spatial selection |
| Extended | Coherent slab generation and reduced timing kernel | Assumes achromatic profiles and adequate full collection |
| Full | Chromatic focal fields and coherent collection through a specified aperture | Retains assumptions about illumination, material response, geometry and selected signal band |

These are physical descriptions, not single Boolean switches. Use
[the forward model](forward_model.md), [geometric smearing](../howto/geometric_smearing.md)
and [collection aperture](../howto/collection_aperture.md) for the corresponding
API configuration. Numerical convergence must be checked separately for every
chosen quadrature.

## Retrieval versus substrate thickness

The revised Gaussian series has fifteen thicknesses from 1 to 40 µm and a
0.5 mm collection aperture. Its archived on-axis reference FWHM is 1.0529 fs.
The full model retrieves 1.046531–1.079009 fs, with processed, frequency-scaled
trace residuals of 0.0045–0.0195%. These ranges describe the tested simulations;
they are not confidence intervals on an experimental pulse.

```{figure} ../_static/paper/thickness_retrieval.png
:alt: Reference and reconstructed traces, common-plane pulse profiles and the complete Gaussian thickness ladder.
:width: 95%

Revised Gaussian thickness series. All pulse comparisons use the on-axis
entrance convention. Full-model duration markers use the archived temporal
sampling; the standard and extended fields are converted before duration is
evaluated. The collection aperture is 0.5 mm.
```

The standard model gives approximately 1.36–2.41 fs after conversion to the
same plane, despite residuals below 0.54%. The extended model captures much of
the propagation and timing distortion, but the restrictive aperture retains
chromatic selection that a frequency-independent kernel omits.

A fresh 64-fold zero-padded transform of the same full-model fields gives
1.045898–1.075489 fs against a reference of 1.052478 fs. This small change is
an interpolation effect, not a new retrieval or uncertainty estimate. Field-plane
conversion is a separate operation and can have a larger effect.

### Retrieval protocol and package defaults

The principal draft protocol uses an objective $R+0.03P_s+0.03P_a$, with a soft
auxiliary spectral constraint and amplitude smoothness. It uses neither
phase-only retrieval nor fitted thickness, delay offset or smearing scale.
The corrected simulated traces receive no defringing or baseline subtraction.
Model delay centring uses the response-weighted marginal at each evaluation,
including its derivative. Initialisation and lowest-residual selection are
recorded per case.

Those settings are **not package defaults**. Gradient solvers currently default
to `reg_spectrum=0.01` and `reg_amp=0.03`; LM uses a differently normalised
objective and different weights. See [regularisation](../howto/regularisation.md)
and [retrieval theory](retrieval_theory.md). Experimental backgrounds and noise
require their own evidence-based preparation, rather than automatic adoption
of a noise-free simulation protocol.

## Structured pulses and dispersion metrology

Phase-structured fields test more than a transform-limited duration. For imposed
GDD of ±2 fs², the full model gives +1.975 and −1.991 fs² at 9.5 µm in the
recorded protocol. However, the negative-GDD case at **2 µm fails**: the selected
result has about −0.98 fs² GDD and a large spurious TOD despite a small residual.
At 40 µm the fitted values are approximately −2.43 and +1.88 fs². The Gaussian
series' duration accuracy therefore does not imply uniform phase accuracy.

```{figure} ../_static/paper/structured_retrieval.png
:alt: Structured pulse profiles and phases, including the unsuccessful negative-GDD retrieval at 2 micrometres.
:width: 95%

Structured-input tests, with the failed thin-substrate negative-GDD case retained.
GDD cases use spectral weight 0.03. The available +2 fs³ TOD example at 9.5 µm
uses weight 0.01 and is a separate protocol. These scans retain their original
softened aperture rendering, unlike the corrected Gaussian and RDW scans.
```

The TOD example gives about 2.04 fs³ for an input of 2 fs³ and preserves its
main trailing structure. It is not a matched-protocol thickness study. FWHM,
polynomial phase coefficients, temporal profiles and complex-field errors answer
different questions; use more than one when truth is known. FWHM can change
abruptly when a satellite or shoulder crosses half maximum.

## Beam geometry: the smearing kernel is quantitative

In the achromatic, fully collected approximation, local arrival times reduce to
a gate splitting $p$ and a probe-minus-gate-centroid offset $\vartheta$. For an
ideal circular-hole BOXCARS mask, their widths scale with $(d/D)\lambda/c$;
the focal length cancels because angle and focal radius change inversely.
The [smearing guide](../howto/geometric_smearing.md) derives and applies these
coordinates, including their correlation.

Gate splitting is not generally an additional duration or a delay convolution.
For a transform-limited **Gaussian**, it attenuates the gate without changing
its shape; a non-Gaussian transform-limited field can change shape. A chirped
Gaussian also produces a spectral shear. The offset coordinate translates the
trace along delay. Their joint distribution is needed to describe both effects.

```{figure} ../_static/paper/smearing_examples.png
:alt: Split gates for Gaussian and non-Gaussian transform-limited fields, and a correlated Gaussian trace example.
:width: 95%

Illustrative calculations from the forward-model draft. Only the Gaussian gate
has a time-independent attenuation ratio. These examples demonstrate the
assumptions behind the reduction, not a universal deconvolution formula.
```

## The collection aperture: two validated routes

Finite collection acts on the complex spatial signal before its squared modulus
is integrated. It can change delay-dependent trace structure, so a fitted
spectral response cannot generally replace it. Opening the aperture reduces
this selection, but also approaches the incident beams and can admit pump
leakage. The [collection guide](../howto/collection_aperture.md) explains the
geometry and clearance limit.

In the tested RDW ladder, full-model temporal-profile errors at the restrictive
0.5 mm aperture are about 0.012–0.045; the extended model reaches 0.123 and the
standard model 0.277. At 2 mm, full and extended errors are closer (about
0.012–0.046 and 0.011–0.037). These are peak-aligned, peak-normalised intensity
rms errors over ±6 fs, not fractional errors in duration. They support both a
more complete collection route and a calibrated finite-aperture model for
these cases, without establishing a universal aperture threshold.

## A simulated single-cycle pulse

The RDW input comes from a simulated hollow-capillary source and is then
propagated through the simulated TG-FROG instrument. Neither stage is an
experimental measurement. Its structured spectrum and satellites provide a
more demanding profile comparison than a Gaussian.

```{figure} ../_static/paper/rdw_retrieval.png
:alt: Simulated RDW trace and reconstructed profiles, with temporal-profile error ladders for two collection apertures.
:width: 95%

RDW comparison at 9.5 µm and a 2 mm aperture, plus the available thickness
ladders. Complex fields are converted to the on-axis entrance convention before
transformation. The lower panels use the common ±6 fs profile metric.
```

At this cell, finely sampled full-model and reference durations are about
1.052 and 1.045 fs. The profile rms errors are 0.0149 (full), 0.0124 (extended)
and 0.2387 (standard). The extended model is slightly better by this particular
metric, so the example does not support universal superiority of the full model.

## Fixed-field validation and numerical convergence

The forward-model draft holds the input field and geometry fixed and compares
predictions with sixteen corrected propagation traces: Gaussian and RDW fields,
four thicknesses from 4 to 40 µm, and 0.5 and 2 mm collection apertures. It
reports both a single global gain and fitted gains for individual frequency rows.
The former retains spectral-yield information; the latter allows an unknown
frequency response. Neither fits the pulse or delay origin.

```{figure} ../_static/paper/trace_validation.png
:alt: Fixed-field model predictions, signed residuals, marginals and thickness dependence for global and row gains.
:width: 95%

Known-field forward predictions using refined quadrature. Global-gain and
row-gain comparisons are separate validation standards. The residual colour
scale is shared within each case, and marginals are not independently rescaled.
```

The refined full model gives 0.032–0.200% rms/peak with a global gain and
0.015–0.178% with row gains. Complete-trace relative-$L^2$ errors are
0.42–2.48%. These metrics differ because the trace occupies only part of the
sampled rectangle. The complete-trace comparison includes the paraxial
$\omega/n$ source-field factor and the declared detector convention; row gains
can absorb a missing spectral factor and therefore cannot validate it.

Increasing focal, depth and collection quadrature together changes the model
by at most $2.806\times10^{-4}$ rms/peak after a global scale. A separate
endpoint test extending the focal radius changes the refined prediction by
about $1.13\times10^{-6}$. These are numerical sensitivity checks for the tested
cases, separate from uncertainty in the propagation reference and from residual
physical model error.

## Solvers and reproducibility

Compare starts, iteration budgets and supported solver routes to assess
optimisation sensitivity. A warm start can help, but the retained negative-GDD
failure shows that accurate forward modelling does not remove every difficult
inverse case. Lowest-residual selection is a specified protocol, not proof of
field accuracy. The [algorithm gallery](../tutorials/02_choosing_an_algorithm.md)
illustrates solver behaviour; the [developer guide](../developer_guide/validation.md)
describes derivative and implementation checks.

## What certifies a retrieval

Report the measurement model, retained data support, weights, gains, constraints,
reference plane, initialisation and stopping rules together with the residual.
Show pulse and spectral profiles, not only a duration. If an auxiliary spectrum
entered the objective, its agreement is a consistency check rather than an
independent validation. Spread between solver starts measures optimisation
sensitivity; it is not measurement uncertainty. Repeated measurements and
calibrated perturbations supply different evidence. See
[uncertainty](../user_guide/uncertainty.md).

## Limits on medium thickness

The demonstrated thickness ranges are conditional on the tested pulse families,
geometry, material, sampling and auxiliary spectrum. They establish no universal
physical thickness ceiling. At larger thickness, numerical resolution, inverse
conditioning, focal evolution and omitted material response all require renewed
assessment. The present scalar, instantaneous-response models do not include a
delayed Raman response or general polarisation dynamics. An older reconstruction
failure cannot establish that either is the limiting mechanism.

## Reproducing

The [gallery](../examples/index.md#instrument-effects-and-validation) runs the
bundled thickness-series and RDW scripts against local reduced datasets. These
are useful, self-contained illustrations with their own preprocessing and model
settings. They do **not** reproduce the revised draft protocol or its complete
corrected datasets. The gallery identifies their provenance and limitations.
Documentation builds use only repository data and cached outputs; they do not
require the private manuscripts or a research-data download.
